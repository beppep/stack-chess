Puzzles

Capture the king in the given number of turns. Resolving is part of your turn. "Mate in 2.5" means you get two turns, and then your opponent has to let it resolve and lose. "Mate in 4" means you let it resolve on your fourth turn and take the king. The computer defends as well as it can, and any move that still wins counts.

The rules

Try the interactive tutorial →
  • Moving puts the move on the stack. It shows as an arrow. Nothing on the board changes yet.
  • Your opponent can respond by spending their turn on a move of their own. It goes on top of the stack. Then you can respond to that, and so on.
  • Or let it resolve. The stack resolves from the top down: the last response first, the original move last. Then it's your normal turn.
  • Fizzle. If a move is illegal when it should resolve, it fizzles and nothing happens.
  • One move per piece. A piece that already has a move on the stack can't be given another one until the stack resolves. Those pieces are dimmed.
  • No check rules. You may move into check, leave your king attacked, or castle through attacked squares. You win by capturing the king, and your opponent can respond to save it. There is no checkmate.

That’s the game!

There are no check rules. You win by capturing the king!
